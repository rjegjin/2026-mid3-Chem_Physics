1. Representations

  • Definition — a homomorphism from the group into $GL(V)$
  • Faithful and unfaithful representations
  • Equivalent representations — related by a similarity transformation
  • Any representation of a finite group can be made unitary

2. Reducibility

  • Invariant subspaces
  • Reducible — can be brought to block-diagonal form
  • Irreducible — cannot be broken down further
  • Maschke's theorem — representations of finite groups decompose completely into irreducibles

3. Schur's Lemma

  • First lemma — a matrix commuting with an irreducible representation is a multiple of the identity
  • Second lemma — a matrix intertwining two inequivalent irreducibles must be zero
  • Proofs of both
  • These are the only ingredients the GOT needs

4. The Great Orthogonality Theorem

$$ \sum_R \Gamma^{(\alpha)}_{ij}(R)^{*}\,\Gamma^{(\beta)}_{kl}(R) = \frac{h}{\sqrt{l_\alpha l_\beta}}\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl} $$
  • The complete proof from Schur's lemmas
  • What orthogonality means here — orthogonal vectors in an $h$-dimensional space
  • Corollary — the number of irreducibles equals the number of conjugacy classes
  • Corollary — $\sum_i l_i^2 = h$

5. Character Orthogonality

$$ \frac{1}{h}\sum_R n_R\, \chi^{(i)}(R)\,\chi^{(j)}(R)^{*} = \delta_{ij} $$
  • Characters are invariant under similarity transformation, which is why they can serve as labels
  • Character orthogonality is the trace version of the GOT
  • Deriving the reduction formula used throughout Module 02
  • Constructing a character table from nothing

6. Why Chemistry Cares

  • The condition for $\int \psi_a^* \hat{O} \psi_b \, d\tau$ to be non-zero
  • → $\Gamma_a \otimes \Gamma_O \otimes \Gamma_b$ must contain the totally symmetric representation
  • IR and Raman activity, the Laporte rule and MO symmetry matching all follow from that one sentence
  • Modules 03, 06 and 09 use nothing but this corollary
