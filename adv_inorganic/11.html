This is the crossing from inorganic into organometallic chemistry. The counting practised here is the only analytical tool used in Modules 12 and 13.

1. Two Counting Conventions

$$ N_e = d^{\,n} + \sum(\text{ligand contributions}) $$
  • The ionic (oxidation-state) method — remove ligands as ions
  • The covalent (neutral) method — remove ligands as neutral fragments
  • The two always give the same total electron count — a discrepancy means an arithmetic error
  • Where the oxidation state is the useful number and where the total count is

2. The 18-Electron Rule

  • Its origin — filling nine valence orbitals ($s$ 1 + $p$ 3 + $d$ 5)
  • Recovered from Module 08 — six bonding pairs plus a filled $t_{2g}$
  • Where it holds — low oxidation states, ligands rich in $\pi$ acceptors
  • Where it breaks — early transition metals (sterics), square-planar $d^8$ (16 electrons)
  • Cycling between 16 and 18 electrons is the essence of catalysis

3. The Ligand Zoo

  • $\mathrm{CO}$, $\mathrm{CN^-}$, phosphines — two-electron $\sigma$ donors
  • Hydride, alkyl, halide — one electron (covalent) or two (ionic)
  • Alkenes and alkynes — the $\pi$ bond is what donates
  • Cp and allyl — multi-site coordination
  • Reference table — contributions side by side in both conventions

4. Hapticity

$$ \eta^n\text{-ligand}, \qquad \eta^5\text{-}\mathrm{C_5H_5} $$
  • $\eta$ counts the contiguous atoms bound to the metal
  • $\eta^5$-Cp (five electrons, covalent count) against $\eta^1$-Cp (one)
  • $\eta^3 \rightleftharpoons \eta^1$ slippage — a common way to open a coordination site
  • Distinguishing $\kappa$ (donor-atom notation) and $\mu$ (bridging)

5. Dewar–Chatt–Duncanson Model

  • $\pi_{\text{alkene}} \to d_{\text{metal}}$ — $\sigma$ donation
  • $d_{\text{metal}} \to \pi^*_{\text{alkene}}$ — $\pi$ back-donation
  • As back-donation grows the C=C lengthens and its substituents bend away from the metal
  • The limit — describing the fragment as a metallacyclopropane
  • $\nu_{\mathrm{CO}}$ as the measurable index of back-donation
  • Zeise's salt — made in 1827, structurally understood only in 1953

6. Representative Complexes

  • $\mathrm{Ni(CO)_4}$, $\mathrm{Fe(CO)_5}$, $\mathrm{Cr(CO)_6}$ — counting practice
  • Why $\mathrm{V(CO)_6}$ exists as a 17-electron species
  • Ferrocene — the sandwich structure and its MO diagram
  • History — the 1951 misassignment of ferrocene and its correction (Wilkinson and Fischer, Nobel 1973)

7. Key Competencies

  • Count any organometallic complex by both conventions and reconcile them
  • Distinguish oxidation state from $d$-electron count
  • Read a hapticity label and assign the electron contribution
  • Compare electron density at a metal centre from $\nu_{\mathrm{CO}}$
