1. Two Ways to Add Angular Momenta

$$ \hat{H}_{SO} \approx \lambda\, \mathbf{L}\cdot\mathbf{S} $$
  • $LS$ (Russell–Saunders) — couple $\mathbf{L}$ and $\mathbf{S}$ first, then $\mathbf{J} = \mathbf{L}+\mathbf{S}$
  • $jj$ — couple each electron's $\mathbf{j}_i = \boldsymbol{\ell}_i + \mathbf{s}_i$ first, then $\mathbf{J} = \sum_i \mathbf{j}_i$
  • Which scheme applies is decided by the size of $\lambda$ against electron repulsion, nothing else
  • Intermediate coupling lies between them — and that is where the real 4d/5d metals sit
  • $\lambda$ grows roughly as $Z^4$

2. Spin–Orbit Splitting

$$ E_J = \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\left[J(J+1) - L(L+1) - S(S+1)\right] $$
  • The Landé interval rule — adjacent spacings scale with $J$
  • Normal and inverted multiplets — less than or more than half-filled shells
  • The $p_{1/2}$, $p_{3/2}$ notation and fine structure
  • Why $J$ becomes a genuinely good quantum number in the lanthanides

3. Magnetism Beyond Spin-Only

$$ \mu_{\text{eff}} = \sqrt{4S(S+1) + L(L+1)}\ \mu_B $$
  • Quenching of orbital angular momentum — the ligand field spoils $L_z$
  • When quenching is incomplete — $T$ ground terms
  • Why spin-only works for 3d metals and fails for the lanthanides
  • The Curie law and temperature dependence
  • The Zeeman effect — level splitting in an applied field

4. Relativistic Effects

  • Core electrons approach $c$ → mass increase → $s$ and $p_{1/2}$ contraction
  • Contracted $s$ shields better → $d$ and $f$ expansion and destabilisation
  • Spin–orbit splitting itself grows
  • All three follow from one cause and then compete with one another

5. Four Consequences You Can See

  • The colour of gold — the $5d \to 6s$ gap narrows into the blue. In silver it stays in the ultraviolet
  • Mercury's melting point — a contracted, stabilised $6s$ participates poorly in metallic bonding
  • The inert-pair effect in $\mathrm{Pb}$ — $6s^2$ stays put, making $\mathrm{Pb(II)}$ more stable than $\mathrm{Pb(IV)}$
  • The lead–acid battery — calculations (2011) attribute most of its voltage to relativistic effects

6. Key Competencies

  • State the conditions under which $LS$ or $jj$ coupling applies
  • Order fine-structure levels using the Landé interval rule
  • Predict when the spin-only formula will fail
  • Explain the anomalies of gold, mercury and lead from a single cause
