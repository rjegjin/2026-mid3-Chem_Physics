1. Hilbert Space

  • A complete vector space equipped with an inner product
  • $L^2$ — why square-integrable functions are the natural stage
  • Orthonormal bases and the completeness relation
  • Where the finite- and infinite-dimensional cases actually differ for chemistry

2. Bra–Ket Notation

$$ \langle \phi | \hat{A} | \psi \rangle $$
  • Kets $|\psi\rangle$ and bras $\langle\phi|$ — vectors and dual vectors
  • Inner products and the projection operator $|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$
  • Reading a matrix element in this notation
  • The overlap integrals and Hamiltonian matrix elements of Modules 05–06 are exactly these

3. Operators and Observables

  • Hermitian operators $\hat{A}^\dagger = \hat{A}$ — real eigenvalues
  • Unitary operators $\hat{U}^\dagger\hat{U} = 1$ — inner products preserved
  • Symmetry operations are unitary — this is the key
  • Change of basis and the invariance of the trace

4. Commutators

$$ [\hat{A}, \hat{B}] = \hat{A}\hat{B} - \hat{B}\hat{A} $$
  • Commuting operators possess simultaneous eigenstates — with proof
  • Non-commuting operators and the uncertainty relation
  • $[\hat{L}^2, \hat{L}_z] = 0$ but $[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y] \ne 0$ — the basis of Module 04 §5
  • The notion of a Casimir operator

5. Symmetry Operators

  • From a geometric operation $R$ to an operator $\hat{R}$ acting on functions
  • $\hat{R}f(\mathbf{r}) = f(R^{-1}\mathbf{r})$ — why the inverse appears
  • $\hat{R}$ preserves the group multiplication structure
  • That correspondence is precisely what Bonus 03 calls a representation
