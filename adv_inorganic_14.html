Lecture 14: From Two Orbitals to a Band

Outline only — prose not written

0. Scope

Central question

How does the MO picture extend to the band structure of a solid?

Requires

Modules 05–06 — MO theory · Module 01 — symmetry

Feeds

Module 15 — every case study speaks the language of bands

This module in one line. $\text{AO} \to \text{MO} \to \text{many MOs} \to \text{bands}$. No new physics — the same MO theory taken to $N \to \infty$.

1. Crystal Structures

  • Unit cell and lattice — a lattice point is not an atom
  • The seven crystal systems and fourteen Bravais lattices
  • Packing fraction and coordination number — simple cubic, BCC, FCC, HCP
  • The number and size of tetrahedral and octahedral holes
  • Ionic structures — NaCl, CsCl, zinc blende, fluorite, rutile
  • The radius-ratio rules and where they fail

2. Lattice Energy

$$ U \propto \frac{M\,z^+z^-}{r_0}\left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right) $$
  • The Madelung constant $M$ — a number fixed by the structure alone
  • The Born–Landé and Born–Mayer expressions
  • The Born–Haber cycle — lattice energy from experiment
  • The gap between calculated and experimental values as a measure of covalency

3. Reciprocal Space

  • The reciprocal lattice — why it is needed at all
  • What the $\mathbf{k}$ vector means — a phase relationship, not a wavelength
  • Brillouin zones and their boundaries
  • Restating the Bragg condition in reciprocal space
  • Only the minimum — enough to read a band diagram

4. From MOs to Bands

$$ E = E(k) $$
  • A chain of $N$ atoms — $N$ molecular orbitals forming a band
  • Band width is set by overlap — $s$ bands are wide, $d$ bands narrow
  • From the bonding bottom to the antibonding top
  • Density of states — inversely related to band width
  • The Fermi level

5. Metals, Semiconductors, Insulators

  • A partly filled band → a metal
  • A filled band with a small gap → a semiconductor
  • A filled band with a large gap → an insulator
  • Direct against indirect gaps — why Si makes no LED and GaAs does
  • Mott insulators — where band theory fails and electron correlation wins

6. Doping

  • Intrinsic carrier concentration and its temperature dependence
  • n-type — donor levels just below the conduction band
  • p-type — acceptor levels just above the valence band
  • The p–n junction and the origin of rectification
  • Degenerate doping and transparent conducting oxides

7. Key Competencies

  • Count formula units and coordination numbers in a given unit cell
  • Obtain a lattice energy from a Born–Haber cycle
  • Carry an MO diagram continuously into a band diagram
  • Classify a solid as metal, semiconductor or insulator from its band structure
