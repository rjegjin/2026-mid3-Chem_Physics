Method, not memory. No cycle is presented as a picture to learn. Every step is tracked in a five-column table: oxidation state, $d$-electron count, total electron count, coordination number, step type.

1. What Makes a Cycle

  • A catalyst is not consumed — the circle must close
  • Distinguishing the catalyst precursor from the active species
  • Turnover number and turnover frequency
  • The rate-determining step and the accumulating resting state
  • Deactivation — dimerisation, metal precipitation, poisoning

2. Hydrogenation — Wilkinson

$$ \mathrm{RhCl(PPh_3)_3} $$
  • A 16-electron $\mathrm{Rh(I)}$ active species
  • Oxidative addition of $\mathrm{H_2}$ → $\mathrm{Rh(III)}$
  • Alkene coordination → insertion → reductive elimination
  • Selective for terminal alkenes, sluggish with tetrasubstituted ones
  • Asymmetric hydrogenation — BINAP, Noyori (Nobel 2001)

3. Hydroformylation

$$ \mathrm{RCH{=}CH_2} + \mathrm{CO} + \mathrm{H_2} \longrightarrow \mathrm{RCH_2CH_2CHO} $$
  • Around ten million tonnes a year — the largest homogeneous catalytic process
  • The cobalt process against the rhodium process
  • Linear/branched selectivity — decided by the regiochemistry of insertion
  • Controlling it with bulky phosphite ligands

4. Monsanto and Cativa

$$ \mathrm{CH_3OH} + \mathrm{CO} \longrightarrow \mathrm{CH_3COOH} $$
  • $\mathrm{[Rh(CO)_2I_2]^-}$ — oxidative addition is rate-determining
  • The role of the $\mathrm{CH_3I}$ co-catalyst
  • Insertion → CO coordination → reductive elimination → hydrolysis
  • Cativa — changing to iridium moves the rate-determining step
  • Same cycle, different metal, different bottleneck — that comparison is the point of this section

5. Cross-Coupling

  • The shared skeleton: oxidative addition → transmetalation → reductive elimination
  • Suzuki boron · Negishi zinc · Kumada magnesium · Stille tin
  • Heck — no transmetalation; it turns on insertion and $\beta$-H elimination
  • Ligand design — bulky electron-rich phosphines assist both terminal steps
  • History — Heck, Negishi and Suzuki, Nobel 2010

6. Reading Any Cycle

  • The five-column tracking template
  • Around a closed cycle the oxidation-state changes must sum to zero
  • When a vacant site is created and when it is filled
  • Exercise — take an unfamiliar cycle and label each step

7. Key Competencies

  • Name the elementary step at each stage of a given cycle
  • Fill in oxidation state, electron count and coordination number for every stage
  • Identify the rate-determining step and the resting state
  • Predict which step a ligand change will accelerate
