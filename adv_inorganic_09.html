0. Scope

Central question

Requires

Module 10 — spin–orbit coupling · Module 15 Case 4 — luminescent complexes

Two sessions are allotted to this module. Spin–orbit coupling is deliberately excluded and deferred to Module 10; everything here is non-relativistic.

1. Microstates

$$ \text{microstates} = \binom{2(2\ell+1)}{n} \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; d^2:\ \binom{10}{2} = 45 $$
  • Counting the allowed $(m_\ell, m_s)$ combinations of a $d^{\,n}$ configuration
  • Recovered from Module 04 — a microstate is a Slater determinant
  • Arranging the 45 microstates of $d^2$ in an $M_L$–$M_S$ table
  • The procedure for peeling terms off that table

2. Russell–Saunders Coupling

$$ \mathbf{L} = \sum_i \boldsymbol{\ell}_i, \qquad \mathbf{S} = \sum_i \mathbf{s}_i, \qquad \mathbf{J} = \mathbf{L} + \mathbf{S} $$
  • The light-atom approximation — electron repulsion dominates spin–orbit coupling
  • How to read the term symbol $^{2S+1}L_J$
  • $L = 0,1,2,3,4 \to S, P, D, F, G$
  • The terms of $d^2$: $^3F, {}^3P, {}^1G, {}^1D, {}^1S$ — degeneracies summing back to 45

3. Hund's Rules for Terms

  • Maximum $S$, then maximum $L$, then $J$ (smallest for a less-than-half-filled shell)
  • Why the ground term of $d^2$ is $^3F_2$
  • Caution — the rules are reliable only for the ground term. Do not use them to order excited terms

4. Free Ion to Ligand Field

$$ ^3F \;\longrightarrow\; {}^3T_{1g} + {}^3T_{2g} + {}^3A_{2g} $$
  • How free-ion terms split in an octahedral field
  • The splitting is governed by $L$ — an $S$ term does not split, an $F$ term splits into three
  • Correlation diagrams between the weak-field and strong-field limits
  • Hole–electron equivalence — $d^{\,n}$ against $d^{\,10-n}$, octahedral against tetrahedral

5. Selection Rules

$$ \Delta S = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad g \nleftrightarrow g $$
  • Spin selection rule — transitions changing multiplicity are forbidden
  • Laporte rule — in a centrosymmetric complex $d$–$d$ transitions are forbidden
  • Why colour is seen anyway: vibronic coupling breaks the symmetry
  • $T_d$ has no centre of inversion, so tetrahedral complexes are far more intense
  • A table of molar absorptivities — the quantitative trace of how forbidden a transition is
  • Charge-transfer transitions evade both rules, hence their very deep colours

6. Orgel Diagrams

  • A qualitative correlation diagram, restricted to terms of the same multiplicity
  • The symmetry between $d^1$/$d^6$ and $d^4$/$d^9$
  • Limitations — no spin-forbidden transitions, no strong-field regime

7. Tanabe–Sugano Diagrams

$$ \frac{E}{B} \quad\text{vs}\quad \frac{\Delta_o}{B} $$
  • Why both axes are scaled by the Racah parameter $B$
  • The convention of laying the ground state along the horizontal axis
  • The vertical discontinuity in the $d^4$–$d^7$ diagrams — the high-spin/low-spin crossover
  • From two or three measured absorption bands to $\Delta_o$ and $B$ simultaneously
  • The ratio to the free-ion $B$ — the nephelauxetic series
  • Worked example — interpreting the spectrum of $\mathrm{[Cr(H_2O)_6]^{3+}}$

8. Key Competencies

  • Count the microstates of a $d^{\,n}$ configuration and resolve them into terms
  • Write the ground-term symbol
  • Explain why a given transition is weak or intense using both selection rules
  • Extract $\Delta_o$ and $B$ from a Tanabe–Sugano diagram
